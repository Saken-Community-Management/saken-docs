# What is Saken

Saken (`saken.com`) is a **shared ledger and budgeted-operations tool** for residential
communities — low-rise buildings and small complexes in Lebanon, 5–100 apartments,
where one resident volunteers to run the community's finances.

It started **web-first** and still is: the mobile-shaped web app at `saken.com` is where
every community lives today. Since then it has grown a **native React Native app** (built
and running on EAS, [not yet in a store](/introduction/status)) and an internal
**[admin console](/admin-console/overview)** that Saken's own operators use to fix things
the product deliberately can't fix for itself. All three read one shared database through
one shared API — see [Architecture overview](/architecture/overview).

## The problem it replaces

> Today this runs on a WhatsApp group, a notebook, and trust. The admin carries the
> building's whole financial picture in their head. Residents don't know what they owe.
> Late payers aren't visible. There's no way to plan for the year, allocate costs
> fairly, or hand the role off cleanly.
>
> — *Saken-v1-Design-Specification.docx, §1 Locked Scope*

Saken is simultaneously a **transparency tool for residents** (they can see what they
owe and where money went) and a **convenience tool for the admin** (a place to plan the
year, track spending against a budget, and run collections without recreating the
picture each month). The admin role is part-time and unpaid; the app's job is to make
it manageable.

## What v1 does

From the locked v1 scope, Saken does twelve things:

1. **Set up the community** — a wizard captures structure (single building or complex)
   and auto-generates the apartment grid.
2. **Set an annual budget** — a fixed list of categories, each with `rate × quantity`
   line items, auto-divided across apartments by share.
3. **Amend the budget** anytime — every amendment writes a dated, versioned snapshot;
   recalculation is forward-going only.
4. **Manage apartments and shares** — shares are proportional weights (nominally out of
   1000) at the complex level.
5. **Sign admins up easily** — Google, email magic-link, phone OTP, or (added later)
   email + password with an OTP verification step.
6. **Record payments and log expenses** — admin directly, or concierge submits for
   approval.
7. **Manage the concierge** — a restricted, Arabic right-to-left surface that can only
   submit expenses.
8. **Approve concierge submissions** — the admin reviews, edits, approves, or rejects.
9. **Create special collections / projects** for one-off costs outside the annual
   budget.
10. **Roll over the fiscal year** — cash carries forward; surplus can reduce next year's
    collection.
11. **Reset the fiscal year** if needed (destructive, with a typed-confirmation gate).
12. **Export PDF reports** suitable for the building's WhatsApp group.

## Who runs it: roles

Saken started with **three roles** and grew a fourth:

- **Admin** — a resident with full edit rights who runs the books.
- **Resident** — read-only view of their apartment, the dashboard, and every other
  apartment's status.
- **Concierge** — a restricted Arabic-UI role that can only submit expenses for
  approval.
- **Treasurer** — added after the original spec; shares the admin's financial powers
  (record payments/expenses, approve submissions) without being the community owner.

Roles are **stackable** and **assigned by the admin** (at `/dashboard/team`), not claimed
by invite link: one person can hold admin *and* treasurer in the same community, and their
effective permissions are the union of what those roles grant. See
[Roles & role stacking](/concepts/roles).

Above all of these sits a fifth, non-product tier: the **platform superuser**, who
operates the [admin console](/admin-console/overview) across every community. No community
member ever sees it.

## Pilot target & success criterion

The pilot targets **2–5 communities** (buildings or complexes), 5–100 apartments each.
The success criterion is deliberately concrete:

> 2–5 real communities use it for one full fiscal year — budget set at the start,
> payments and expenses logged throughout, year-end PDF generated. If that happens, v1
> worked.

## What v1 explicitly does **not** do

Online payments or bank integration · tenant-vs-owner distinction · any non-USD currency ·
system-calculated late fees · a late-payer warning flag · voting/decision logs · in-app
announcements or chat · cash reconciliation/variance/handover logs · savings pots beyond
special collections · resident self-signup · custom dashboards/report templates · full
edit-version history of payments/expenses.

Four items from that original exclusion list have since been built anyway, and are worth
knowing about because the locked-scope document still lists them as out:

| Originally out of scope | What actually shipped |
| --- | --- |
| A single user in multiple communities | Multi-community with an active-community pointer — see [Multi-community](/concepts/multi-community) |
| An Arabic UI for residents and admins | Full EN/AR localisation across the whole app via next-intl (cookie locale, no URL routing). Arabic strings are still awaiting native review |
| Push notifications | The mobile app registers devices and receives payment-reminder pushes |
| A native app | `saken-mobile` is built and on EAS, though not yet published |

<Callout type="info">
**Currency & language**

Saken is **USD-only** — every amount renders with a `$` prefix, and money columns are
`NUMERIC(12,2)` end-to-end (never floats). Every surface is available in **English and
Arabic**; the concierge surface defaults to **Arabic, right-to-left**. The locale is a
cookie (persisted to `users.language` once signed in), not a URL segment — so every route
path in these docs is the real one in both languages.
</Callout>
